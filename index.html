Idea-first fiction

Somebody had to think of it. That was the hard part.

A neopulp is a book a person imagined and directed, and a machine wrote at their instruction. The vision, the taste, the judgment of what stays and what goes — those are the author's. The sentences are the machine's. The idea is the authorship. It always was.

A human vision, perfected.
The promise every neopulp makes
Why · A new medium

The machine frees the imagination from the work of execution.

01 · Writing

Before writing, ideas lived only in speech. Written down, an idea could travel without its thinker.

02 · The press

Before print, every copy was made by hand. The press made copies cheap and readers plentiful.

03 · The internet

Before the internet, publishers decided what got published. The web let anyone reach readers directly.

04 · The machine

Until now, having the idea and writing the book were one job. The machine splits them: the idea is enough.

For all of history, most creativity died in the gap between conceiving a thing and being able to make it. The vision was there; the ten thousand hours were not. Having the idea and writing it down were the same job, so the books belonged to the people with the hours — not the people with the ideas.

That gap just closed. What's left is the part that was always human and always the hard part — the idea, the taste, the direction, the nerve to follow one vision all the way to the end. The job moves up a level: not every letter, but the shape of things — proportions, flows, where it's going and why. This is not the death of creativity. It's the release of it. Creativity was never scarce; the means were.

Be honest about what follows. The marginal cost of a new book is falling to zero. Anyone can write anything and put it anywhere — and most ideas suck. Not all ideas are equal, and execution still matters: a machine is a reflection of what you put into it. The results depend on the person directing it.

That is what the mark is for. In a flood of infinite books, it tells a reader that a person initiated this, directed it, understood what they were making, and aimed at something good — said on the front page, without hedging, because a new medium is nothing to apologize for. It never was. Ask the camera.

Two years from now, this is simply how books get made. Nobody gets points for having been pissy about it. Learn how the world spins now, and look back proud — not for making the best of it, but for making more of it.

The imagination is free. Come see what it makes.

01

Idea-first fiction

A book that exists because a person imagined it. Not a genre — a way of deciding what a book is for. The vision carries the weight the execution used to. And it should be fun: the medium is finally cheap enough to try things in.

02

Said out loud

Every neopulp names how it was made, on the title page, in the same size as the byline. Not a disclaimer at the back. A credit at the front.

03

Nobody's brand

There's no publisher here. The mark is free to apply to your own book if you keep the promise and print the credit. That's the whole membership test.

“It's a
neopulp.”

Lowercase, with an article, like a novel, a zine, a podcast. Plural: neopulps. The word is a common noun and belongs to everyone using it — that's the point, and it's why nobody trademarks it.

WHY IT'S FIRSTA disclosure at the back reads as something you were caught at. At byline scale on the title page it reads as what it is — a claim on the part that was hard.

WORDS TO AVOID“AI-assisted”, “co-created”, “in collaboration with”, and anything that begins “While some may…”. Hedging is the only thing that actually looks like shame.

Apply the mark

Three rules, then it's yours to use
RULE 01

Keep the promise

A human vision, perfected. The machine is a reflection of what you put into it — so direct it, frame it, and rewrite until the book converges on the vision. If it pads, drifts, or gives up halfway, the mark is a lie and readers will price it in.

RULE 02

Print the credit

“Seeded by [name]” on the title page, at byline scale, with the two sentences underneath. Front of book, not back.

RULE 03

Don't redraw it

Use the supplied files. The keyline and wordmark may take your cover's text colour; the acid square never changes and the stamp never rotates. Full rules →

SVG or PNG?

SVG for anything printed or resized. PNG at 4× for slides, social, and storefronts that reject vectors. Never scale a PNG up.

Before the printer

Convert type to outlines. Widths are pinned in the SVG so lockups hold their proportions even where Archivo isn't installed — but outlines are safer.

Typefaces
